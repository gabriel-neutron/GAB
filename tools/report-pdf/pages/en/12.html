For the buyers who stayed loyal, this fall is a windfall. Refiners in India, China and Turkey keep buying Russian crude through intermediaries, and the pressure of sanctions on its price improves their margins.31 China, in particular, has paid less for this crude than for that of its other suppliers since 2022. Moscow thus yields part of its oil rent to China, because it cannot lose its first customer.32

2.3 The budget

Oil taxation in Russia is based on the Urals price.33 A bigger discount therefore cuts what the state, which pays for the war, draws from each barrel: a 10% discount on sales to China alone costs the Russian Treasury about $3 billion a year.34
