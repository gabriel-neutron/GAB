2.2 The price and the cap

To measure what Russia loses on each barrel, the report compares its oil with Brent, the North Sea oil that serves as the reference price. Urals, the main Russian crude exported, is heavier and higher in sulphur, so a little cheaper. In 2019, before the war, this price gap, called the discount, averaged less than $1 a barrel from January to September.22 Since December 2022, an EU company or a company of its allies can no longer carry, insure or finance Russian crude sold above a price cap (figure 4).23 The coalition chose a cap rather than a full embargo because it pursues two aims at once: reducing the oil profits that let Russia wage the war, and keeping world energy markets stable and well supplied.24
