5 Who owns what

Behind every port, every tanker and every bank stands an owner: sometimes the state, sometimes a person close to power, sometimes nobody who can be named. Who holds these nodes, and how far does a sanction reach into them?

A sanction does not hit only the companies it names. In the United States, a company owned 50% or more, in aggregate, by designated persons is blocked as well.95 The EU applies a test of 50% or more, in aggregate, and the United Kingdom a test of more than 50% that adds control.96 Knowing who owns a company therefore tells how far a sanction reaches it. The report traces each chain of shares up to the state, to a person, or to a company with no documented ultimate owner (figure 12).

5.1 The state and its holdings

At the top, the state holds the large companies through a few holdings. A public holding that it owns outright is the first shareholder of Rosneft and, together with the Federal Agency for State Property Management (Rosimushchestvo), gives the state close to half of Gazprom.97 Gazprom in turn owns almost all of Gazprom Neft98 and is a main shareholder of Gazprombank.99 Likewise, the state holds all the voting rights of Transneft, which owns the holding of the port of Novorossiysk.100 A designation of any of them therefore reaches its subsidiaries owned 50% or more.
