# Drafting

Gabriel does not write prose. It produces the material and the references; the analyst
writes the report elsewhere.

## Why this is out of scope

This is a boundary of purpose, not of difficulty. Gabriel's job is to make claims
traceable — every assertion carries its source, and the evidentiary layer exists so that
what leaves the system can be defended. Generated prose is the exact opposite: fluent,
unsourced, and persuasive in proportion to how little it is checked.

A drafting feature would also blur the one distinction the system depends on. Readers who
do not understand the candidate/evidentiary split are already a declared risk; handing them
machine-written narrative built from both layers would make that split invisible.

What Gabriel owes the report is structure and citations. Search across documents, graph and
internet, conversational drill-down from any element, and an evidentiary layer whose every
claim points at a document — that is the material. Turning it into sentences is the
analyst's judgement and the analyst's signature.
