# GAB design

## Style

GAB is an investigation tool. Its interface is precise and quiet. Reports combine an editorial reading flow with figures that let the reader inspect the evidence.

- Use a dark Obsidian ground or a light Bone ground for reading and print. Keep interface controls neutral. Use colour to explain data, selection and evidence states.
- Use Geist for titles and prose, and Geist Mono for labels, dates, units and values. Keep text readable and the hierarchy clear.
- Build layouts with grids, thin rules, square corners and deliberate spacing. Avoid shadows, blur, decorative gradients and nested cards.
- Give each figure a clear purpose. Keep its units, period, source and limits close to the data. Never invent values or relationships to fill a layout.
- Keep reports compact enough to compare figures, with room for prose and captions. Adapt the composition to small screens.
- Use motion to explain a change or guide attention. Keep text clear of ASCII backgrounds. Show a stable final state when reduced motion is requested.
- Treat ASCII text and image treatments as experiments for reports. They must preserve reading, evidence and navigation.

The references below support exploration. They do not require a change to every page or the use of every component.
